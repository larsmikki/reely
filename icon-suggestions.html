Fetchr · icon exploration, round 4

Same polish, own composition

Last round copied the reference's actual construction — chip, legs, inset die, corner badge — which was reusing it, not just learning from it. What's worth taking from that example is the density of detail and layering: several small elements working together with real shadow and depth, instead of one flat glyph. The three concepts below aim for that same level of finish, but each is built from Fetchr's own vocabulary — color-coded collections, the persistent scrubber, fetching from a URL — not from hardware-emblem parts.

fetchr — fanned card stack
at 16px: color tab and duration chip fade; the fan + play triangle still read as "a library," not just "a video."
Concept 1

Card Stack Library

Three fanned thumbnail cards with real cast shadow, not a single frame. The front card carries the two details that are actually unique to Fetchr's UI: a color tab (collections are color-coded) and a duration chip (every card shows a runtime). It's an icon of the product's own front page, not a generic play button.

Concept 2

Scrub & Fetch

A play frame sits above its own progress bar, complete with a scrub handle — this is Fetchr's persistent player, which is the feature that actually separates it from a plain gallery app. The small interlocked-link mark top-left stands for "pulled in from a URL," worked into the frame itself instead of hung off the corner as a separate badge.

frame: #ec4899 → #be185d · track: #3a2530 · handle: white / #be185d core
fetchr — sprocketed cell + pulse

Concept 3

Filmstrip Pulse

A single film cell, real sprocket perforations punched along both edges, with a live-signal pulse trace running underneath — "a film, currently streaming," rather than the neural-net-style abstract diagram from last round. Most old-media of the three; worth weighing against Fetchr streaming primarily web video, not literal film.

strip: #ec4899 → #be185d · sprockets: #1c1015 punched · pulse: white @ 65%
All three keep a dark plate purely so the layered detail shows up clearly in this comparison — that's a display choice, not a proposal to darken Fetchr's actual brand tile. If one of these directions holds up, the next step is re-testing it against Fetchr's real full-bleed pink gradient background to make sure the detail still survives without the contrast boost a dark plate gives it for free.
